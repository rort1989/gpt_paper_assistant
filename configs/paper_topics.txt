 1. New architectures or training methods for unified multimodal models that perform both understanding and generation in a single model, especially for video. This includes shared or joint latent representations across perception and synthesis, multi-task frameworks that bridge the two, and recipes (pre-training, instruction tuning, RL) for training one model to both interpret and produce multimodal content.
    - Relevant: papers that explicitly propose a "unified", "any-to-any", "omni", or "understanding and generation" model, that share a tokenizer/latent space/backbone between comprehension and synthesis, that analyze interference or synergy between the two objectives, or that introduce training strategies for jointly optimizing them.
    - Not relevant: papers that only do understanding (captioning, VQA, retrieval) or only do generation (text-to-video, text-to-image) without a shared model or representation. A model that merely calls a separate generator as an external tool is not unified.
 2. Using generative models or generative priors to improve understanding, i.e. generation-enhanced understanding. This includes world models used for video reasoning and future prediction, diffusion or autoregressive generative pre-training as a backbone for downstream perception tasks, and using synthesis as an auxiliary objective that improves comprehension.
    - Relevant: papers showing that generative pre-training, generative features, or a learned world model measurably improves downstream understanding (recognition, grounding, temporal reasoning, planning, QA); papers on world models for video prediction when the goal is reasoning or evaluation of dynamics.
    - Not relevant: generation papers that only report generation quality; using a generative model purely as a synthetic-data factory for a narrow task, with no claim about generative priors or representations.
 3. Using understanding or semantic signals to improve generation, i.e. understanding-enhanced generation. This includes injecting recognition, grounding, depth, layout, or scene-graph understanding into video generation, controllable generation driven by scene understanding, and using understanding modules as critics or verifiers to enforce temporal, physical, or identity consistency.
    - Relevant: papers that add a perception/understanding module (VLM, detector, tracker, captioner, reward model) into the generation pipeline or training loop to improve controllability, prompt adherence, or consistency; papers that use understanding-based feedback or rewards for generative models.
    - Not relevant: conditional generation on low-level signals alone (edges, pose, depth maps) with no semantic understanding component; standard classifier-free guidance or text-encoder swaps.
 4. Efficiency and scalability of unified or multimodal understanding-generation models. This includes teacher-student knowledge distillation from expert understanding or generation models into a smaller unified model, efficient architectures for resource-constrained or on-device deployment, and compression such as quantization, pruning, sparsity, caching, or step distillation.
    - Relevant: papers reporting concrete efficiency gains (latency, memory, FLOPs, parameter count, sampling steps) for multimodal understanding and/or generation models, and distillation setups where expert models supervise a unified or smaller student.
    - Not relevant: generic efficiency work on text-only LLMs or on image classifiers with no multimodal generation or understanding component; pure systems/kernel engineering with no model-level contribution.
 5. Evaluation of unified multimodal and video models: new metrics, benchmarks, or human evaluation methodology. This includes benchmarks that jointly test understanding and generation, metrics for generation quality that correlate better with human judgment, and protocols for reliable human or model-based judging.
    - Relevant: papers introducing a benchmark, dataset, metric, or evaluation protocol for multimodal understanding, video generation, or both together; analyses of the validity, contamination, or reliability of existing metrics and leaderboards.
    - Not relevant: papers that merely evaluate on existing benchmarks, or that release a dataset solely as training data with no evaluation protocol.
 6. Technical reports and model cards for frontier models, in particular multimodal or video language model releases. These describe the architecture, data, training and post-training recipe, and evaluation of a newly released model.
    - Relevant: technical reports for newly released frontier or near-frontier models, especially multimodal, video, or any-to-any models, including open-weight releases that document data curation, training infrastructure, and post-training.
    - Not relevant: papers that only fine-tune or evaluate someone else's released model; marketing-style system descriptions with no architectural, data, or training detail.
 7. Agentic workflows and agent fine-tuning applied to video, covering video editing and content creation in long and short formats as well as video understanding or reasoning. This includes multi-agent or tool-using pipelines that plan, script, shoot, edit, and revise video, and training agents (SFT or RL) to use video tools or to reason over long videos.
    - Relevant: papers with an explicit agent loop (planning, tool calls, memory, critique, self-revision) whose task is producing, editing, or analyzing video; agentic video editing and end-to-end content-creation pipelines (storyboarding, shot selection, cutting, captioning, soundtracking, repurposing long video into shorts); papers that fine-tune or RL-train a model to act as such an agent, including training it to call editing tools or NLE APIs; long-video reasoning via agentic retrieval over frames or clips.
    - Not relevant: single-pass prompting of a VLM with no agentic structure; general-purpose agent benchmarks with no video component; classic non-learned video editing software; single-shot video-to-video or instruction-based editing models with no agent loop (those belong under criterion 3).
 8. Research on what makes short-format video compelling, and on user engagement with it. This includes modeling or predicting engagement, retention, and virality, and using such signals to guide generation or curation.
    - Relevant: papers that measure or model viewer engagement, watch time, retention, hook strength, or aesthetic/narrative appeal of short videos, including engagement-aware reward models or rankers, and empirical studies of what drives engagement.
    - Not relevant: generic recommender-system papers about click-through on non-video items; video quality assessment that only measures distortion or technical quality with no link to engagement.

 In suggesting papers to your friend, remember that he enjoys papers on statistical machine learning, Bayesian models, and generative modeling.
 Your friend also likes learning about surprising empirical results in language models, as well as clever statistical tricks.
